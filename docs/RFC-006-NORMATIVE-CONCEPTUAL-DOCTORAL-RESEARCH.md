# RFC-006 — DOCTRINA v0.4 Research Specification
## Normative–Conceptual Legal Dissertation: Claim Typology, Method Warrant, and Internal Thesis Examination

**Date:** 25 November 2026  
**Status:** methodological research proposal — **not implemented**, **not validated**  
**Baseline:** DOCTRINA v0.3, RFC-005  
**Scope:** an *ongoing doctoral dissertation in normative legal research using conceptual inquiry*. Not a generic article writing assistant, generic RAG platform, or claim to determine doctoral eligibility.

> **Core thesis of this RFC:** DOCTRINA should evaluate the *inferential fitness of an author's developing thesis* against its chosen jurisprudential inquiry. It must not use a single doctrinal, positivist, moral-philosophical, or purely deductive standard for every dissertation. The final originality assessment remains the last review stage; this does not postpone reading prior work.

---

## 1. Literature-based methodological findings

### 1.1 Three coordinates must be independently specified

DOCTRINA v0.3 sometimes speaks of `normative`, `doctrinal`, and `conceptual` as if they named a single uniform method. They do not. In Indonesian methodological convention, *penelitian hukum normatif* may name the overarching research orientation and *pendekatan konseptual* an approach within it; in international scholarship, *doctrinal* inquiry into the law according to its sources and *normative* criticism by extra-legal standards may be deliberately distinguished. The classification is not harmonised across traditions.

For **each inquiry**, record:

1. **Research orientation:** what legal phenomenon / normativity is being studied, and the jurisdiction/time if positive law matters.
2. **Inquiry aim:** doctrinal articulation, conceptual clarification, jurisprudential explanation, interpretation, internal critique, external normative evaluation, or warranted reconstruction.
3. **Argumentative method:** what counts as a successful inference, what counts as relevant evidence, and how disagreements are adjudicated.

An author may combine aims; each shift requires a transition warrant. The vocabulary of any one scholar must not be presented as universal consensus.

**Sources:** Van Hoecke (ed., 2011); Smits (2017); Bix (1995); article `Carefully Tailored` (2025) explicitly distinguishes a source-bound doctrinal perspective from external normative evaluation, but does so using a *positivist assumption* the author acknowledges. Hage (2010/2011) models a *different*, much more external normative conception and is useful precisely as a warning against equating labels.

### 1.2 Conceptual inquiry has different success conditions

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, *The Nature of Law*, separately discusses conceptual analysis, metaphysical reduction, metanormative inquiry, prescriptive ethics, and constructive interpretation. These are **distinct philosophical programmes**, not interchangeable procedural steps. Bix (1995) likewise highlights different purposes of conceptual jurisprudence that can lead theorists to talk past one another.

Thus no blanket instruction to `reconstruct the law` or `construct a new theory` should be imposed. A rigorous *immanent critique* can expose a concept's instability without writing a new statute; a Dworkinian constructive interpretation asks different questions from a Raz-inspired conceptual analysis, and a Derridean reading may deliberately examine unresolved tensions.

### 1.3 Doctoral standards assess sustained argument, not word count or format alone

UGM's doctoral law guidance requires contribution to legal knowledge, critical thinking, deep subject mastery, persuasive argument, correct methodological application, and scientific integrity. Oxford Law's DPhil progress review separately tests feasibility and methods (proposal) and a **sustained, well-structured academic argument** (written sample). EUI Law states that method must fit the chosen question and that a topic must be focused enough to support a dissertation.

**Design consequence:** each chapter has an *argumentative contract*. The chain `conceptual problem → claim(s) → warrants → chapter-specific contribution → central thesis` is more important than a universal Chapter I–VI skeleton. Institutional chapter shapes belong to the university profile.

## 2. Revised claim ontology (semantic contract for later implementation)

Every claim must record **what kind of truth or justification** it asserts, not just the string `normative`.

| Code | Claim category | Question it answers | Required defense |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-SEM | semantic / definitional | What does this term mean in a specified usage? | Textual context, stipulation, interpretive reason; distinguish *definition* from actual validity. |
| C-CON | conceptual / constitutive | What makes something an instance of legal concept X? | Explicit features/conditions, hard cases, rival conceptions, category boundaries. |
| C-ONT | ontological / explanatory | What explains the existence or grounding of legal entity or norm X? | Explanatory scope, assumptions, rival accounts; no automatic prescriptive entailment. |
| C-DOC | doctrinal / positive-law | What does law in jurisdiction J at time T require, permit, prohibit or empower? | Identified authoritative sources, valid time/status, interpretive warrant, competing constructions. |
| C-INT | interpretive / constructive | What reading best justifies or explains the practice/text? | Interpretive commitments, source fit, justification criterion, alternatives (as warranted by method). |
| C-NOR | normative / evaluative | What ought to be required or preferred and why? | A defended normative standard, applicability, objections, institutional consequences when relevant. |
| C-CRI | critical / genealogical | What internal tension, exclusion, contradiction or contingent construction is exposed? | Fair target reconstruction, exact textual location, immanent or external critical criteria; **no mandatory positive replacement**. |
| C-EMP | empirical / causal | What happens, how often, or with what actual effects? | Empirical evidence appropriate to the claim; conceptual analysis alone cannot establish frequency or causal impact. |

In a single paragraph, claims can belong to several categories. Each is examined under its own conditions, *then* the transition is examined. Recording `C-NOR` is not itself a logical error.

### 2.1 Five high-risk transitions (new proposed checks)

**NT-01: legal definition → legitimacy.** A state's capacity to classify an activity as public interest does not by itself establish or refute its substantive moral or political legitimacy.

**NT-02: legitimacy → legal validity.** Lack of moral legitimacy does not automatically establish invalidity of a legal act under a particular system; state the theory/constitutional doctrine and operative rule, if making that move.

**NT-03: conceptual property → legal remedy.** Explaining the nature of personhood, sovereignty, public interest or rights does not automatically yield a concrete remedy without a normative/institutional bridging argument.

**NT-04: doctrinal source → universal philosophical claim.** A statute, judgment or set of cases demonstrates a bounded legal position; it does not by itself prove a necessary feature of law across all possible legal systems.

**NT-05: historical/genealogical dependence → present invalidity.** Revealing the contingent origin of a concept is not automatically a reason to treat all applications as legally or morally unjustified.

The above are **review hypotheses** only; explicit interpretivist or non-positivist theories may supply warrants that justify a transition. This is *not* a hidden rule that positivism always prevails.

## 3. Operational protocol for a dissertation currently being written

### A. Intake without rewriting the researcher

Require the actual draft passage or argument description and its scope; identify whether text is authorial, reported doctrine, quotation, opposition argument or AI proposal. Record unavailable chapters as **unknown**, not as successful or failed.

### B. Build a Conceptual Research Charter

Record exact contested concept, competing **conceptions**, question type, jurisprudential method, institution/time where relevant, scope limits, concepts author explicitly accepts, and theory roles (object of criticism, interpretive lens, normative basis, comparative opponent, methodological tool). No compulsory grand/middle/applied theory ladder.

### C. Reconstruct thesis at the correct scale

For the excerpt: produce exact claim anchors and a faithful prose explanation. Distinguish **author-asserted premises** from **AI-hypothesized hidden premises**. Map the chapter's argument to the dissertation's central thesis, not to a generic outline.

### D. Internal argument audit (primary loop)

Run, in order:
1. **Target fidelity:** Is this the author's own view or one attributed to an opponent?
2. **Concept identity:** Are concept, conception, definition and normative ideal distinguished?
3. **Claim type / evidence adequacy:** Does each statement have an appropriate kind of support?
4. **Inferential warrant:** Do premises justify this claim under its chosen inference mode?
5. **Normative bridge:** If descriptive/doctrinal claims yield an ought, what gives normative force to the conclusion?
6. **Theory-role continuity:** Has a philosopher's position silently shifted from being an object of criticism to the thesis's authority?
7. **Coherence across chapters:** Does the author preserve domain, timeframe, concepts, and explicit commitments?
8. **Boundary conditions:** What would count as a counterexample or scope limit? For immanent/deconstructive work, identify whether an aporia is a *finding* rather than an error to eliminate.

**Output** at least one Argument Review Card specifying the textual basis, judgment category, strongest objection, smallest possible repair, researcher's required decision and downstream impact. A review can remain open rather than fabricating a solution.

### E. Dialectical challenge only after faithful reconstruction

Test the strongest rival under a defensible reading; include a steelman of that opponent. An argument that exposes another theory's contradiction must first reproduce that theory accurately from inspected primary texts. Distinguish rebuttal, undercutting warrant, limiting qualification and unresolved disagreement.

### F. Revision propagation and doctoral integration

Every approved change to a major definition/premise/warrant triggers impacted subchapter/chapter re-review. Chapter contracts should identify: **what the reader must now be justified in accepting, what remains unproved, and which subsequent chapter depends on it**.

### G. Defense rehearsal, then **final** originality review

The researcher defends key commitments. *Only once* internal + dialectical + integration + defense review is provisionally stable may a contribution comparison be issued. Prior literature is researched throughout A–F: **novelty verdict is deferred, not literature review**.

## 4. Research-specific audit examples (all hypothetical)

### Example 1 — Public interest (`kepentingan umum`)

Original synthetic move: "The state defines public interest; therefore this definition cannot represent society; therefore all takings under that definition are legally invalid."

- C-SEM/C-DOC: state definition (requires specific jurisdictional legal materials to be a positive-law claim).
- C-NOR/C-CRI: representational legitimacy.
- C-DOC: legal invalidity.
- Flags: NT-01 + NT-02 + IA-10 + IA-13. A defensible repair may narrow the thesis to *formal designation alone does not settle substantive justification*. **This is only a proposed repair** and is not yet a legal claim about any current Indonesian statute.
- If the author insists on universal invalidity, request the decisive legal/normative warrant rather than rewriting it away.

### Example 2 — Legal personhood

Original synthetic move: "A machine can be explained as holding rights and duties, therefore it should receive full legal personhood."

- Ontological/constitutive and normative claims have separate justificatory burdens.
- Ask which incidents of personhood are necessary, why legal policy should confer them, and which objections to the model remain.
- The conceptual conclusion and the normative conclusion can both be plausible **without one entailing the other**.

### Example 3 — Aporia / deconstruction

Original synthetic move: "A legal concept requires an exclusion that it cannot fully justify under its own claimed universality."

- If this is an immanent critique, first identify the concept's own *claim to universality* and the excluded case.
- A genuine unresolved tension is **not** automatically IA-05 or a methodological failure.
- It is a serious failure only if the critique misattributes the doctrine's commitments or draws a conclusion beyond what its text demonstrates.

## 5. Current v0.3 implementation gaps to correct

The existing `scripts/audit_argument_graph.py` is a deterministic JSON linter, not a semantic dissertation examiner. Several limitations are material:

1. `kind` is too broad; `normative` conflates evaluative judgments and different normative modalities, while `conceptual` is not distinguished from semantic or ontological explanations.
2. IA-06 treats an asserted `normative_bridge: true` as structural presence, even when the actual warrant is indefensible. A boolean cannot prove an argument.
3. It detects dependency cycles, but cycles can be legitimate hermeneutic or reflective-equilibrium structures.
4. It detects declared `contradicts` edges but not whether the author's text really contradicts itself, nor whether positions are merely quotations of opponents.
5. The stage-ready fields are author/system declarations, not independent proof of scholarly examination.
6. There is no systematic concept/conception register or a law-version-bound legal-validity/legitimacy distinction.

**Policy for v0.4:** do not claim these are fixed by drafting RFC-006. Implement claim-ontology changes in a *versioned schema*, preserve v0.3 fixtures via migration, add explicit `author_or_opponent` and `source_inspection` fields, and evaluate against human-annotated real drafts before speaking of semantic precision.

## 6. Validation plan (not yet executed)

**Corpus:** researcher-authorized excerpts from ongoing doctoral normative–conceptual dissertations, representing at least:
- analytic–doctrinal clarification;
- interpretive–constructive argument;
- critical–genealogical analysis (including successful negative conclusions);
- normative–reconstructive proposal.

**Reviewers:** at minimum two researchers with jurisprudential/method expertise for adjudicating serious disagreements; a substantive-law specialist for jurisdiction-specific claims. Preserve disagreement, instead of forcing labels into a single pseudo-objective score.

**Test families:** 
- T-CON: concept vs conception / description vs construction;
- T-NOR: defective or defended normative bridges;
- T-THEORY: author vs opponent; theory-role drift;
- T-INT: interpretive fit and rival readings;
- T-CRI: legitimate aporia, immanent critique, and false-positive circularity;
- T-CHAPTER: cross-chapter change impact;
- T-SOURCE: correct quote but unsupported interpretation; version of applicable law;
- T-ORIG: final audit held until earlier gates stable, while earlier literature exploration remains permitted.

**Measures:** reviewer agreement on *where the argument is*; critical-finding precision and recall against adjudicated labels; correct location anchors; false positives on legitimate non-deductive or critical inquiry; repair fidelity judged by researcher; and proportion of revisions causing appropriate downstream re-review. No arbitrary numerical target is a doctoral standard.

**No source or textual data of an actual unpublished dissertation should be committed to a public repository without express authorisation.**
