# DOCTRINA — Internal Argumentation Fault Taxonomy

These labels are **review hypotheses**, never automated judgments that a thesis is false.

| Code | Failure hypothesis | Test | Caution / permitted exception |
|---|---|---|---|
| IA-01 | Missing argumentative warrant | Can the stated premises justify the conclusion? What linking principle is relied on? | An implicit but reconstructible warrant may be legitimate; label system reconstruction. |
| IA-02 | Circular justification / petitio principii | Is a conclusion needed to justify one of its own premises? | Hermeneutical circularity or reflective equilibrium can be methodologically legitimate; graph cycle only prompts review. |
| IA-03 | Scope shift | Has meaning, time, jurisdiction, ontology or category shifted between premises and conclusion? | Explicit justified scope extensions may be permissible. |
| IA-04 | Equivocation | Does the same term do argumentative work in inconsistent senses? | Distinguish polysemy being *studied* from uncontrolled equivocation. |
| IA-05 | Contradictory commitments | Do two author-endorsed propositions conflict under matching conditions? | Dialectical staging, quotation of opponents, and different historical periods are not contradictions. |
| IA-06 | Normative bridge deficit | Does an ought/value prescription arise solely from empirical, semantic or descriptive facts? | If a normative/interpretive premise is genuinely supplied, assess its adequacy rather than auto-reject. |
| IA-07 | Authority and method overreach | Is a doctrinal, statutory, philosophical, or empirical claim made with sources/method that cannot establish it? | Distinguish normative propositions from claims about actual outcomes. |
| IA-08 | Theory-role drift | Is a theory initially an object of critique but silently treated later as an authoritative premise? | The researcher may change roles if the transition is justified and recorded. |
| IA-09 | Counterexample vulnerability | Can a plausible hard case defeat or restrict an unqualified proposition? | Negative/deconstructive inquiries need not supply universal counter-rules. |
| IA-10 | Non sequitur / underdetermination | Do the premises allow multiple incompatible conclusions, with no reason for preferring this one? | Some arguments are defeasible and comparative rather than deductive. |
| IA-11 | Strawman / internal misrepresentation | Does critique actually address an opponent's strongest position, according to inspected primary texts? | Treat inaccessible source passages as unverified. |
| IA-12 | Buried premise | Does the argument rely on an assumption introduced as settled earlier but never defended? | May be an explicit research assumption, but scope and cost must be stated. |
| IA-13 | Overgeneralization | Is a limited case, statute or conceptual example used to claim universality? | Some conceptual claims are universal by argument, not empirical extrapolation. |
| IA-14 | Redundant argument or chapter | Does a section provide no new step in sustaining or qualifying the central thesis? | Deliberate recapitulation may serve clarity but is not a new argument. |
| IA-15 | Incompatible methodological commitments | Are incompatible philosophical criteria combined without explaining their different analytical levels? | Productive tension may be the subject of the thesis; don't enforce harmony. |
| IA-16 | Source-text misfit | Does a quotation exist but not support the author's interpretation or paraphrase? | Exact text checks are necessary but insufficient. Requires expert inspection. |

## Six-level audit depth
1. Sentence/proposition: meaning, modality, domain, type.
2. Paragraph: premises, inferred conclusion, warrant, qualifier.
3. Subsection: thesis progression and dependency.
4. Chapter: argumentative task, objections, contribution to central thesis.
5. Dissertation: compatibility, dependency, changing assumptions across chapters.
6. Revision: downstream impact of changed claims/theory/method; re-run reviews.

## Inference forms
`deductive`, `defeasible`, `analogical`, `interpretive`, `abductive`, `critical`, `normative-constructive`, `genealogical`.
Not all can be assessed by a truth-functional consistency checker. Use Toulmin reconstruction as a scaffold, not a universal ontology of argument.

## Review severity (qualitative)
- **Foundational:** central thesis fails without revising/removing key inferential step.
- **Major:** essential subargument insufficiently defended; plausible repair available.
- **Moderate:** clarify scope, evidence or definition before finalizing chapter.
- **Editorial:** wording, signposting, duplicate exposition only.

A problem can be `open`, `responded`, `conceded`, `reframed`, or `deferred_with_justification`; "responded" is not automatically "resolved".
