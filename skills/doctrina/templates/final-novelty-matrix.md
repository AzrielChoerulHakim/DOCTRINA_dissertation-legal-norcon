**Caution:** material distinction alone is not sufficient; the new claim must have a justified legal-conceptual or normative contribution. The limits of search and access must be disclosed. Consider the dissertation author's own and coauthored previous publications.

## Type-specific examination

### If primary = normative
- What is proposed or clarified normatively? (criterion, construction, remedy, ordering, reformulation):
- Is it grounded in actual sources and defended normative premises?
- Would the proposed consequences follow in the selected legal system, taking exceptions and affected rights into account?
- Has an equivalent normative proposition already been made by others?

### If primary = interpretative
- Exactly which norm or concept is being interpreted? At which source/version?
- What interpretive methods and textual/systematic reasons are offered?
- What is the strongest available alternative interpretation and why does this interpretation perform better under the author's justified standards?
- What legally meaningful consequence differs?

### If primary = conceptual
- Which identified legal concept and its usages are examined?
- What conceptual ambiguity, boundary error or internal tension is resolved?
- Which hard cases are better explained or clarified?
- What is the conceptual delta relative to prior scholarship, and why does it matter legally?
