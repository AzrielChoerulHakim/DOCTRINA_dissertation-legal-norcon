# Module 09 — Polisilogisme dengan formalisasi simbolik

Use this route when a sequence of explicit legal propositions and warrants can be reconstructed. It is not a demand that every legal argument be a categorical syllogism.

1. Define domain, entities, predicates, operators and each symbol's ordinary-language meaning. Preserve quantifier scope, time, jurisdiction, modality and exceptions. Distinguish descriptions of law, facts, evaluative premises and ought claims.
2. For each premise record precise source/locator, status (inspected, partial, unverified, proposed), acceptance rationale and a defeating condition. Mark hidden premises as hypotheses rather than author commitments.
3. State formal inference type and validity conditions. For deductive moves show derivation; check countermodels, equivocation, invalid conversion, affirming consequent, denying antecedent, scope shifts. For defeasible, analogical, abductive or interpretive moves state a substantive warrant, alternatives, burden, and limitations; never call them deductively valid merely because they use arrows.
4. Chain intermediate conclusions as premises of later steps only within original scope and qualifications. Track IDs, upstream premises, rule and conclusion; audit circularity and missing links. Test sensitivity to removing or revising a load-bearing premise.
5. Separate valid form from soundness, positive-law applicability and normative justification. No is-to-ought without justified normative bridge; a deontic operator is not ordinary implication. Do not collapse obligations, permission, prohibition, power, validity and legitimacy. If necessary use a clearly stated nonclassical or defeasible representation and acknowledge lack of formal completeness.
6. Translate conclusions back into precise legal language and test the strongest rival interpretation and counterexample.

Synthetic demonstration, not positive law: P1 ∀x(A(x)→B(x)); P2 A(a); C1 B(a) by universal instantiation and modus ponens. P3 ∀x(B(x)→C(x)); C2 C(a) by same rules using C1. Define A/B/C and justify both universal conditionals before applying to a legal object. A statutory exception may defeat P1 as formulated; recast the antecedent or adopt a defeasible rule and reopen C1 and C2. A conclusion about what should be done would require a distinct normative premise.

Output table: ID | ordinary-language proposition | formula (or not formalizable) | type | sources | rule/warrant | depends on | exception/objection | status. A proof sketch is not a claim of soundness. Reference notes in references/symbolic-logic-library.md.
