# Module 01 — Research Object: NORMA or KONSEP

**Activate** for dissertation intake or when a currently examined legal norm/concept, research problem or method becomes unstable.

## Mandatory primary object

There are exactly **two accepted primary research objects**:

1. **NORMA (norm)**: an identifiable legal provision, legal norm or legal principle in an identifiable legal system, including hierarchy, interpretation, coherence, scope, conflict, legal status, exceptions and justificatory implications.
2. **KONSEP (concept)**: an identifiable legal concept as deployed in law, doctrine or legal practice, including meaning, legal definitions, boundaries, conflicting uses, internal inconsistencies and normative implications.

Combined studies may involve both but designate **one primary object**. Do not fabricate a statutory provision, concept usage, legal source, effective date, jurisdiction or the author's thesis.

**OUT OF SCOPE:** a scholar's overall theory as the research object, a project whose research question is to prove or falsify an entire theory, or research that requires developing a new general theory. Theories may appear as limited analytical/interpretive tools to address an eligible legal norm or concept.

## Route N — NORMA

1. Locate the precise legal norm and relevant authoritative source, law-in-force timeframe and jurisdiction.
2. Specify the legal problem (conflicting norms; ambiguous scope; source hierarchy; incoherent obligations; doubtful exception; inadequate justification).
3. Reconstruct the author's interpretation and premises without silently strengthening them.
4. Test inferential gaps, competing interpretations, hierarchy, exceptions and normative conclusions against the inspected materials.
5. Explain what follows for the relevant norms and for later dissertation chapters, without claiming an empirical effect without evidence.

## Route C — KONSEP

1. Locate actual uses of the legal concept and its relationship to authoritative norms and doctrine.
2. Distinguish the concept from rival conceptions and particular legal definitions.
3. Specify the conceptual problem (equivocation, boundary problem, internal inconsistency, insufficient conditions, contested normative implications).
4. Reconstruct the argument, test hard cases and alternative interpretations.
5. Evaluate how the proposed conceptual clarification, qualified critique, or norm–concept reconstruction affects the legal system under examination.

## Methods and theories

- State **why** a doctrinal, conceptual, interpretive, immanent-critical or normative-constructive approach fits the question.
- Record any external theory's **instrumental role only**: interpretive lens, source of a specific contested normative premise, or comparison for a particular legal norm/concept.
- Do not judge whether a complete theory is true or false; ensure references to it are fair, precise and qualified.
- Keep legal validity, legitimacy, conceptual meaning, institutional desirability and actual empirical effects distinct.
- A defensible critique of a norm/concept need not end in a proposed legal reform.

## Output and gate

**Output:** Norm/Concept Research Charter with primary object, located sources/usages, the author's question, method warrant, claim-type transitions and dependent chapters.

**Gate:** a philosopher's name, a general methodological label, or an unidentified legal object cannot satisfy the research-object requirement. If the project is outside NORMA/KONSEP, do not process it as an in-scope dissertation.
